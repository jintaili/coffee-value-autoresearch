Coverage check before a full run

The completed second pilot used the earlier seven-question contract on 40 development reviews. JEV selected long, short, or conflicting finish duration for 11; balanced sensory language for 13; a defect for one; and an independent lot competition for one. It selected no lot auction or concrete scarcity claim. Those counts are small-sample observations, not estimates of prediction value. The revised eight-question contract has no extracted answers yet.

The model's supported probability was exactly zero for documented scarcity in all 40 pilot responses. It never chose lot auction; its highest auction-support probability was 0.19. This is little usable variation for those columns in a random pilot, even though a targeted lot sample may differ.

The earlier combined finish-character question selected a positive, negative, or mixed finish for 28 of 40, but a source audit found clear unsupported choices. For example, wildflower-honey notes in the finish were called sweet even though the finish was not described as sweet; a sharp finish was called bitter or drying; and a drying, flower-saturated finish was called mixed without an explicit positive finish quality. Finish length also has clear errors: a drying finish was labeled long and fading in milk was labeled a short finish. The revised contract separates sweet/clean from bitter/drying to make those claims easier to audit, but it has not been validated.

Across the 8,911 eligible review states, a crude text scan found finish or aftertaste mentions in 91.5%, duration terms near them in 23.7%, sweet/clean terms near them in 19.4%, bitter/drying terms near them in 16.1%, and balance language in 31.1%. Defect terms occurred in 1.8%, auction in 2.5%, competition language in 2.9%, and possible concrete quantity language in 0.4%. These keyword counts include irrelevant contexts: 152 of the 227 auction mentions contain the phrase “auction system,” often as boilerplate about Kenya. They are ceilings or rough proxies, not verified labels. Serving-page coverage has not been measured.

Scope recommendation: do not run all eight for the high-price objective. A training-only price audit points to exact-lot auction as the strongest new JEV candidate, plus Panama × Gesha using existing A+B answers. Competition merits a targeted lot-specific audit. Finish and balance language may matter for rating, but show weaker incremental price potential. Defer sensory defects and concrete scarcity for this price experiment.

Price and approval boundary

The same 40 development rows form the pilot. All 8,911 eligible rows form the full backfill, including those 40. A read-only estimate scales previously billed token counts by the ratio of the new and original request sizes, then adds a 25% margin. The revised eight-question request projects approximately $0.0050 for the pilot and $1.1245 for the full set. The two completed seven-question pilots cost an estimated $0.00731 combined, so the projected total is about $1.137. Actual token use may differ. Successful-response estimates do not capture any charges for failed or retried requests.

The approved ceiling was $1.25 in estimated successful-response charges for the earlier seven-question proposal. Fresh approval is needed for this revised eight-question contract. The backfill checks projected spend before starting, stops at its per-run cap, and writes progress to a resumable cache. A bad pilot or unexpected token use stops the full run for review.
