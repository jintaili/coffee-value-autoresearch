Training-only price audit · 22 September 2026

Which JEV claims point to a high price?

A rare supported claim can be useful even when most rows are “not stated.” The question is whether those supported rows are disproportionately expensive and whether JEV can identify them more precisely than the existing text model.

Recommendation: prioritize an exact-lot auction claim. Test Panama × Gesha using the existing A+B JEV answers. Keep exact-lot competition as a second candidate for a targeted audit. The finish, balance, defect, and scarcity questions have weaker evidence for improving high-price prediction.

Why exact-lot wording matters

A manual scan of the 31 strict auction matches found real lot claims, but also at least one explicit “non-auction lot,” a comparison to another record auction, and blends containing an auction component. A JEV question could exclude these cases. The broader corpus has 227 auction mentions, of which 152 contain “auction system,” often generic Kenya background. The lot-specific boundary is more important than the raw coverage rate.

Competition wording also needs a lot-specific boundary. Some Cup of Excellence mentions describe earlier wins by a farm, not this coffee. Much of the apparent price association overlaps Panama Gesha. Documented scarcity has almost no labeled support in this corpus; an explicit missing outcome is sound schema design, but it cannot create positive training examples.

What this does and does not establish

These are exploratory associations on training rows, not feature ablations or causal effects. Keyword matches are imperfect, and the saved model already has text, origin, variety, and package features. Mean underprediction for auction lots does not prove that adding a JEV auction column will improve validation RMSLE. Review prose also differs from serving product pages; comparable page coverage has not been measured.

The next compact test is one exact-lot auction JEV question on a sample enriched for auction and non-auction mentions, plus a price-only Panama × Gesha interaction using existing A+B features. Add exact-lot competition only if a targeted audit establishes clean positives beyond Panama Gesha. Defer the other new questions for this high-price objective.
